## Enforceable rules for the July 2026 pair

- Effective date: 2026-07-27.
- Scope: all non-media product types in covered Amazon stores; the policy page excludes Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and the United Arab Emirates.
- Title limit: 75 characters including spaces.
- Item Highlights limit: 125 characters including spaces.
- Do not use promotional phrases such as free shipping or a 100% quality guarantee.
- Do not use these prohibited title characters: `!`, `$`, `?`, `_`, `{`, `}`, `^`, `¬`, `¦`.
- Use `~`, `#`, `<`, `>`, and `*` only when contextually required, never decoratively.
- Include enough information to identify the product clearly.
- Avoid repeated words and repeated information.
- Use digits rather than spelling out numbers when practical.
- Follow marketplace capitalization and grammar; do not use all caps.
- Use size and color in child-ASIN titles, not parent-ASIN titles.
- Keep subjective or promotional claims out of the title.

Amazon states that noncompliant existing titles may be corrected automatically or suppressed from search. The 2026 announcement also states that titles over 75 characters may be progressively updated to Amazon's AI recommendation.

## Information allocation

The policy limits and content prohibitions above are enforceable rules. The allocation method below is an internal optimization method, not an Amazon ranking formula or disclosed field-weight model.

Use the complete optimized legacy title generated by `amazon-listing-optimization` as the sole reference title. First decompose its existing wording into ordered semantic phrases, then allocate those phrases between Title and Item Highlights as one searchable field pair. Bullets, description, keyword data, user facts, variants and original evidence may validate, prioritize and add directly supported facts, but they are not substitute reference titles.

Required Title identity:

`[Brand] + [natural core product phrase] + [applicable child SKU attributes]`

The remaining verified phrases are selected contextually rather than inserted in one fixed order.

Internal selection priority:

1. Brand and core product identity.
2. Verified `dual_value_differentiator` phrases.
3. Necessary child variation attributes.
4. Other phrases supported both as keywords and selling points.
5. Verified hard specifications or differentiating attributes.
6. Keyword-only phrases.
7. Selling-point-only phrases.
8. Secondary scenario, care, and audience information.

- Make the Title independently understandable on mobile.
- Preserve useful original words, phrases, and relative order from the legacy title when they remain natural and compliant.
- At the same priority, compare legacy-phrase reuse, keyword evidence, purchase-decision value, character efficiency, marketplace-language naturalness, then legacy relative order.
- Do not enforce a reuse percentage. Record each reference phrase as retained, rewritten, moved, or omitted with its reason.
- A differentiator is optional. If verified, score it according to the same priority method; do not force it directly after the core phrase or block the options when none exists.
- Reserve Title space for brand, core product identity, and higher-priority `dual_value_differentiator` phrases before evaluating the complete child SKU set. Keep that complete set in Title when the evaluated result fits within 75 characters; otherwise transfer the complete set to Item Highlights and record the priority-aware character calculation. Parent ASINs omit variation-specific SKU attributes.
- Allow controlled capitalization, punctuation, inflection, grammar, and unit compaction such as `60 x 40 cm` to `60x40cm` when meaning is preserved.
- Rewrite or omit legacy wording that is overlong, repetitive, prohibited, unsupported, or unnatural, and retain the audit reason.
- New wording must be supported by user materials, verified Listing facts, the keyword pool plus a matching product fact, or other reliable evidence. A plausible category inference is not evidence.
- Write Item Highlights as 2-4 comma-separated phrases, not full sentences, when the verified fact density supports that many distinct decision details. Do not pad a fact-poor product merely to reach a phrase count.
- Use Item Highlights for the highest-priority verified phrases not selected for the Title, followed by supported specifications, uses, compatibility, care, or quantified detail. A material or benefit must not be left as an isolated label when direct structural, use, care or performance detail is available.
- Avoid unproductive repetition. A Title attribute may be repeated only when Item Highlights add a supported count, measurement, specification, structure, compatibility, or use detail required to explain it.
- Do not treat an adjective by itself as sufficient cross-field refinement.
- Never include the report delimiter `|` in either Seller Central field.
- Prefer objective, specific wording over broad or subjective wording. Treat `ideal` as subjective rather than a reusable template phrase.
- Treat 74-75 character Titles as valid but warn about limited headroom and prefer a shorter equally strong candidate.

Amazon states that Item Highlights are searchable and appear below titles in search results and on product detail pages. This does not disclose a ranking weight for the field.
